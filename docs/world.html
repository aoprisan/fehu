A world you can play

An economy is a loop: currency goes round and round, and goods are made, moved up a grade and used up. Below, that loop is running — a miniature of fehu-economy's catalogue, recipes, jobs, merchants and world effects — and every coin and every unit you see move is a real posting in a real ledger. There is also a game on top of it. You hold one hand; the world holds the other, plays by the same rules against the same clock, and is trying to finish richer than you.

The two rules of the loop: currency is never created, only moved — the bar under the board is the whole supply and it never changes width. Units are the opposite: a purchase or a job makes them out of nothing and consuming them takes them away for good. They are counted separately because they are counted by a different rule.

The hands

Both play the same four moves. Buying ore takes cents out of your wallet and makes units that did not exist. A job takes its inputs and its cost up front and delivers at an instant fixed when it started — which is why a scandal that lands halfway through does not change what you were promised. Selling is an ordinary order against a merchant that is holding real inventory and real money, and can run out of either.

Why it behaves like this

The money only moves

Every posting is balanced: what leaves one wallet arrives in another, in the same transaction, in integer cents. Buying ore does not burn your money — it puts it in the seam's wallet, which the world sweeps back to the treasury, which funds the merchants, which is where your sale proceeds come from. That is the loop, and it is why the supply bar never moves.

Units are made and used up

Nothing but a command brings a unit into existence: a purchase from the catalogue, or a job delivering. Consuming one takes it out of the world for good and moves no currency at all — a thing that has been used up is not a thing that has been sold. So the unit counts rise and fall while the money goes round.

A promise, and a price

An event pushes two different numbers. A production modifier is read once, when a job starts, and written into the job — so what you were told you would get is what arrives. A demand modifier is read every time a merchant re-quotes, so the world's appetite shows up in the depth of the book, live, while you are deciding whether to sell.

The same seed is the same world

The price path and the events are drawn from one seeded stream, in a fixed order. Restart with the seed still in the box and the world does exactly what it did before — so the only thing that changed the outcome was you. That is the invariant the whole library is built to hold.
